An $80 flip is a sticker, not a wage

A furniture flip looks clean on the receipt: $40 at the estate sale, $120 on the marketplace listing, $80 gross. The listing fee takes three percent of the sale, $3.60, and that is where most ledgers stop. The piece also occupies floor. A 10 by 10 storage unit runs about $150 a month, which is $1.50 per square foot, and a dresser with walking room claims 4 of those feet. That is $6 a month, 20 cents a day, and every day the dresser waits is a line item.

At a 45 day turn the ledger reads: fee $3.60 plus $9.00 of floor rent, $12.60 total, take $67.40, and 84 percent of the gross survives. Let the same piece sit 90 days and the rent doubles to $18.00, costs reach $21.60, the take drops to $58.40, and 27 percent of the gross has gone to the clock. Spread across the floor time, the fast turn earns about $1.50 a day and the slow one 65 cents. The six hour sand and paint that felt free prices at $11.23 an hour on the fast turn and $9.73 on the slow one.

The rule comes straight off the ledger: a $4 price cut only pays if it moves the sale 20 days sooner, because 20 days of floor rent is exactly $4. A 30 percent cut on the $120 listing is $36, and $36 of rent is 180 days, half a year of storage for one piece. The unit is a warehouse with no dock, the daily rate is 20 cents, and the $80 gross is the sticker, not the wage.
